# Master brief generation

A **master brief** (a.k.a. cliff report) answers one question: **where and why did
this show lose its audience?** Output is a single static HTML file in the house
format, generated by a Python script, never hand-typed.

Shows run 300–700 episodes of ~9–10 minutes. The benchmark is always **The Alpha's
Bride (TAB)**, `show_id = cbc35fa13bbe154873ec97f78f2b4cf4b66c7d31`.

## The one rule that matters most

> **Go to the raw data. Form your own diagnosis. Never inherit a prior read.**

Every serious error on this project came from trusting a summary or an earlier
analysis instead of re-reading the episode summaries and the comments. One shipped
brief named the wrong villain because the character was assumed, not traced.
Another called a show a "vampire romance" when the word does not appear until
episode 137.

When you write a causal claim, ask: **did I read the episodes that prove it, or did
I infer it?** If inferred, go and check.

## Run this sequence

1. Unzip the pack; read `NOTES.md` (may be empty — do not rely on it); confirm the show_id.
2. `python toolkit/analyse.py --pack ./unzipped_pack` — parses the CSV, both summary
   formats and every comments layout; detects corrupt LDAU rows; builds the block
   table; classifies every block; prints comment quotes per flagged window; writes
   `analysis.json`.
3. Read the block table **before reading any story**. The numbers decide where to look.
4. Classify every flagged block against the four traps below.
5. Only now read the summaries: the hook, each flagged window, the finale.
6. Trace every causal claim by searching the character name across the whole run.
7. `python toolkit/build_report.py --pack ./unzipped_pack --title "Show" --out report.html`,
   then fill in `LOGLINE`, `LEAD`, `CAST`, `ACTS` and the stitch text at the top of the file.
8. Validate, **render in a browser and look at it**.
9. Re-read your own story column: have you smuggled commentary into it?

**The scripts do the arithmetic. You do the reading.** The classifier tells you
where to look and which windows are artifacts; it cannot tell you why a creative
flag happened.

## The core metric

**Block drop-rate ratio** = the show's mean drop-% in a block ÷ TAB's mean drop-% at
the **same cumulative hour**.

```python
tab_at = lambda H: min(TAB, key=lambda e: abs(hr(TAB, e) - H))
ratio  = mean(dr(SHOW, e) for e in block) / mean(dr(TAB, tab_at(hr(SHOW, e))) for e in block)
```

Match on **hours, not episode numbers** — episode lengths differ between shows.
Blocks of 10 episodes under ~450 episodes, 20 for longer.

| Ratio | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ≤1.0 | holding at or better than the hit's pace |
| 1.3–1.6 | mild |
| **≥1.6** | genuine flag |
| ≥2.5 | severe |

**LEVEL vs RATE — do not confuse them.** Level is the % of the ep-51 audience still
listening; rate is how fast they are leaving right now. A late block can show a huge
ratio simply because TAB is flat by then. Always report both. Baseline the level at
**episode 51**. Compute a survival headline (of 100 listeners at hour X, how many
reach hour Y) from a **clean, non-paywall** window.

Columns you need from the episodic CSV: `show_id`, `Story no.`, `Duration cum. (HR)`,
`LDAU (Modified)` (level), `Drop % (Modified)` (rate, ×100).

## The four traps — all have actually happened

1. **Corrupt LDAU rows.** Level can only fall; any episode exceeding the previous by
   **>12%** is a defect. Real: *The Vampire Prince* ep 270 = 15,019 vs ~7,500;
   *The Wolfless Deviant* ep 183 = 9,311 vs ~4,660 — exact double-counts. Interpolate
   out of the level curve; `Drop %` is usually unaffected, but check.
2. **Paywall / free-window boundaries.** Real: WD 241–250 = **33×** (level 38%→10%);
   VP 551–570 = 6.75× with listeners saying free episodes had stopped (665−100=565 ✓).
   Detection: one block an order of magnitude beyond anything else, plus paywall
   complaints above that show's baseline. A paywall block may show a *low* complaint
   rate — people who hit a wall leave silently; magnitude is the stronger signal.
   **Never let a paywall block drive the diagnosis or the cut point.**
3. **Delivery faults that self-heal.** Real: *The Last Howl* 321–340 ran 2.9× and 3.6×
   with out-of-order episodes, back to **1.08×** by 351. Story breaks do not recover;
   delivery faults do. A spike that reverts within ~2 blocks is an ops fix.
4. **Release dumps.** Real: *Crushed and Crowned* finale zone 5–15×; a comment at
   ep 504 explains ~30 episodes dumped after six months of no free episodes.

**In four of the five shows analysed so far, the single largest number on the chart
was not a story problem.**

## Structure — follow exactly

1. Eyebrow line, `<h1>` show name, subtitle, meta line (episode count, data sources)
2. **Logline** — one paragraph, the premise as if to a first-time reader
3. **Who's who** — 5–6 `castrow` entries
4. **The problem, in one line** — a `lead` block with the headline finding and the
   survival number, plus a `note` box titled **"Checked, not assumed"** listing every
   data correction made
5. **Part I · The story, in full** — a `readnote` explaining the layout, then 8–9 acts
6. **Part II · Why it breaks** — the charts, the evidence table, then the mechanism
7. **Part III · What to do** — "The stitch": two cut-option cards + a "three rules for
   the continuation" note

### The act layout — the part most often got wrong

```html
<section class="act">
  <div class="act-head">
    <span class="act-num">Act 1</span><h3>Title</h3>
    <span class="act-eps">Episodes 1–30</span>
  </div>
  <div class="act-body">
    <div class="story">
      <div class="col-h">What happens in the story</div>
      <p>…</p><p>…</p>
    </div>
    <aside class="aud cliff">
      <div class="aud-h">The audience here</div>
      <div class="aud-status">…</div>
      <p class="aud-ret">…</p>
      <div class="cmts"><div class="cmts-h">In their words</div>
        <blockquote>…<cite>— ep 171</cite></blockquote></div>
    </aside>
  </div>
</section>
```

**The `<aside>` must sit INSIDE `.act-body`.** In the original briefs it was placed
after the closing `</div>`, the grid never formed, and story and analysis ran
together in one unreadable stream. Verify in a browser:
`getComputedStyle(document.querySelector('.act-body')).gridTemplateColumns` should
return two values, e.g. `512px 300px`. **Check at a viewport wider than 900px** —
house.css collapses to one column at `max-width:820px` by design, so a narrow window
reports a defect that is not there.

### Story and commentary must not mix

The left column is **story only** — what happens, told straight, present tense, no
evaluation. "This is the show working", "a genuinely strong twist", "the audience
does not read this as strength" belong in the **panel**. Act headers carry the act
name and episode range only, no diagnostic tag. Aim for **~900–1,200 characters per
act body** (3–4 real paragraphs); two thin sentences per act is the most common
quality failure.

### Charts

Two stacked inline-SVG charts in one `<figure>`. Top: level curve, show vs TAB, both
normalised to their own ep-51 = 100%. Bottom: per-block ratio bars with a dashed
green line at 1.0× ("benchmark pace"). Colour by severity (green <1.3, amber 1.3–1.6,
red ≥1.6) and use a **distinct colour (brown) plus a label** for any paywall/artifact
zone so the chart does not contradict the text. Dashed vertical lines at boundary
episodes; stagger label anchors; give the viewBox enough height that axis labels are
not clipped.

### The evidence table

Titled **"Every flagged block — the evidence table"**, between the charts and the
mechanism. Columns: *Episodes · vs benchmark · What is happening in the story · Type*.
The **Type** column is the point — it separates `creative` from `monetization` from
`post-paywall · not comparable`. Highlight flagged rows.

## House style

Use `assets/house.css` verbatim. Cream editorial aesthetic, Georgia headings. Static
HTML only — **no JavaScript, no interactive charts**. Key classes: `wrap` `eyebrow`
`sub` `meta` `rule` `logline` `sec` `secn` `lead` `note` `two` `card` `part` `castrow`
`act` `act-head` `act-num` `act-eps` `act-body` `story` `col-h` `aud` `aud-h`
`aud-status` `aud-ret` `cmts` `figure`. Format reference:
`examples/EXAMPLE_cliff_report.html`.

## Validate every time

```python
from html.parser import HTMLParser; HTMLParser().feed(html)          # must not raise
import re; assert not re.findall(r'&(?!amp;|lt;|gt;|quot;|#\d+;|nbsp;|#x[0-9a-fA-F]+;)', html)
```

The unescaped `&` is a recurring defect — it comes from titles like "Crushed &
Crowned" and pairs like "Zeke & Alicia". Escape to `&amp;`.

### Before you ship

- [ ] LDAU anomalies found and interpolated?
- [ ] Every large block classified against the four traps?
- [ ] Two-column grid verified in a browser at >900px, not just in the markup?
- [ ] Story column free of evaluation?
- [ ] Act bodies ~900+ characters?
- [ ] Charts consistent with the text (artifact zones distinctly coloured and labelled)?
- [ ] HTML parses; zero unescaped `&`?
- [ ] Every causal claim traced to specific episodes?
- [ ] Survival headline computed from a clean (non-paywall) window?

## Calibration — five shows already verified

| Show | Eps | Verified diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| **The Last Howl** | 465 | Best holder; converges to TAB parity. 321–340 = delivery bug, self-heals to 1.08×. Real exodus **391–420 (2.1×, 3.4×, 2.5×)** when a prodigy infant takes over. |
| **Seduced by Darkness** | 395 | Royal forced-marriage romance (vampire revealed only at ep 137). 0.31× for the whole first half. Breaks **221–230 at 4.6×**: heroine becomes a spectator in Sebastian's backstory, prior love **Alyssa** installed retroactively. Ends in a couple-swap — final 12 episodes belong to Zeke and Alicia. |
| **Crushed and Crowned** | 572 | Reverse-harem; clean to ep 160. Breaks **161–180 (3.9×)**: Olivia severs the bond as revenge on mates just proved innocent. **The forgery was by the triplets' parents and the spell by Anita's father — NOT Damien**, who is the triplets' uncle and rescues her from traffickers. Reckoning ~80 episodes late. Finale zone = release dump. |
| **The Wolfless Deviant** | 427 | Strongest hook, leakiest show — never reaches TAB parity. Worst genuine bleed **151–170 (2.2–2.5×)**, both leads marry other people. **241–250 = 33× = free window closing, not story.** |
| **The Vampire Prince** | 665 | Strongest holder (0.21×). Breaks **291–310 (2.11×)** on trackability — 30-episode flashback plus a hero split into **Gavriel / Gavrael**, indistinguishable in audio. 551–570 = 6.75× = paywall at ~ep 565. |

**The most important calibration case — VP ep 331–490.** The show hands most screen
time to a *second couple* (leads appear in ~39% of those episodes) and retention
**holds** (0.88–1.31×). In Seduced by Darkness the same move was fatal. The difference
is **pause vs replace**: in VP the leads return and own the finale. Never flag a
secondary-couple stretch without checking whether the leads come back.

### Failure modes and measured cost

| Mode | Cost |
|---|---|
| Lead stops driving | 2.1–3.9× |
| Lead becomes a spectator in someone else's backstory | **4.6×** |
| Protagonist identity untrackable (names, timelines) | 2.11× |
| Permanent handoff to another couple | terminal |
| Central relationship stalled by a rival marriage | 2.2–2.5× |
| Central question resolves with nothing to replace it | the vacuum that follows |

All five are romance-driven supernatural serials — a strong prior for Romantasy,
untested elsewhere.

## Limits to state in the output

- Retention is **measured**; story causation is **inferred**. Say which is which.
- A stitch fixes structure, not appeal, casting or promo.
- The block ratio compares against one benchmark show — a good yardstick, not a law.
- If a claim rests on an unread stretch, say so rather than smoothing over it.

**If unsure, prefer the smaller checkable claim.** The most damaging output on this
project was not an incomplete brief — it was a confident, fluent, wrong one.
